Ballot Measure Explainer · Florida Amendment 3

See what Amendment 3 could mean for your property tax bill.

A plain-number look at how the proposed homestead exemption increase compares to what you pay today — using your own assessed value and millage rate.

On the ballot: Nov 3, 2026 Requires 60% voter approval If passed, starts Jan 1, 2027
What changes

The homestead exemption on non-school taxes rises from about $50,000 today to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028.

What stays the same

School taxes are untouched. Your Save Our Homes assessment cap and portability still work exactly as before.

Who qualifies

Homestead property only. Florida residents as of Dec 31, 2026 get the full increase right away; newer residents phase in over 5 years.

The trade-off

Counties and cities lose billions in revenue, which may show up later as higher millage rates or new fees.

Where to find these numbers

You don't need to guess — every number the calculator asks for is already sitting in your mailbox.

A note on format: the walkthrough and example below are based on a Hillsborough County TRIM notice. Pinellas, Pasco, and Hernando counties send the same required information, but the layout, section names, and row order on your notice may look a little different.

  1. 1
    Find your TRIM notice. Short for "Truth in Millage," this is the "Notice of Proposed Property Taxes" your county property appraiser mailed in mid-August. It's stamped "DO NOT PAY — THIS IS NOT A BILL" in the corner — it's not asking for money, it's disclosing next year's proposed rates before the taxing authorities finalize them at public hearings.
  2. 2
    Find "Assessed Value" in the value table. Near the bottom of page one is a table called "Property Appraiser Value Information." It's not the "Market Value" row at the top of that same table — scroll down past the Save Our Homes Cap line to "Assessed Value." It's usually the same figure in every column (County, Public Schools, Municipal, Other), so any one of them works.
  3. 3
    Add up the millage rates, two ways. Above the value table is the "Taxing Authority Tax Information" table, listing each authority with its own rate across a few columns. Add together the two rows under "Public Schools" (School-State + School-Local) for your school millage. Add together every other row — County, Municipal, Water Management, and any special districts — for your non-school millage. Using the rightmost column ("your tax rate this year if proposed budget change is made") gives the closest read on where rates are actually headed.
  4. 4
    No notice handy? Look it up online. Your county property appraiser's website has a free property search where you can pull up a parcel by address and see the same assessed value and exemption details. Quick links for our area are below.
